Abstract

Geometry-enhanced VLN methods build spatial memory for vision-language models by enriching past frames with features from geometry foundation models. However, these frames remain tied to their original geometry and only implicitly related to the agent, limiting spatial intelligence. We observe that streaming geometry foundation models already offer an untapped opportunity to update them as new observations arrive. We propose RevolveVLN, a framework that turns this opportunity into Evolving Egocentric Memory. At each step, it refreshes representations of past observations with the latest streaming geometry and relates them to the agent’s current pose through explicit geometric transformations. Trained only on R2R-CE and RxR-CE with monocular RGB input, RevolveVLN sets a new state of the art under the same training setting; when modestly scaled, it further matches large-scale navigation foundation models like LightNav-0 and Qwen-RobotNav trained on up to 30× more additional data. Furthermore, it achieves zero-shot gains over all models, including GPT-6.1-sol, on NavSpace, a benchmark for spatial intelligence.

Experiments

R2R-CE & RxR-CE · Val-unseen

Strong navigation from benchmark data alone

Trained only on R2R-CE and RxR-CE, RevolveVLN reaches 64.2% and 65.3% success rate, respectively.

Scaling with additional data

1.10M extra samples.
69.3% success on R2R-CE.

With additional training, our 4B model reaches 69.3% SR, compared with 68.5% for LightNav-0 and 66.9% for Qwen-RobotNav.

LightNav-0 uses about 30× as many additional training samples. All three models use single-view RGB.

NavSpace · Zero-shot evaluation

Beyond route following

RevolveVLN reaches 29.1% macro success with benchmark training data alone. With 1.10M additional samples, it reaches 33.7%, exceeding GPT-6.1-sol (high) by 2.9 percentage points.

The macro average gives equal weight to each of the six spatial task types.
